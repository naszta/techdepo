## Overview

The Helion H7 1470 is a twenty-core, twenty-eight-thread LGA1700 processor from the Harrier
generation, with eight performance and twelve efficiency cores. It boosts to 5.5 GHz and has
integrated graphics. Its base TDP is 125 W, but under sustained all-core load it draws up to
about 253 W, which is the number to plan cooling and power around.

Choose a 280 mm or 360 mm AIO or a large dual-tower cooler, and a power supply with generous
headroom. It runs DDR5 up to 5600 MT/s or DDR4 up to 3200 MT/s, depending on the board, and
there is no cooler in the box.

## Who it's for

Enthusiasts who game at high frame rates and also render, encode or compile, and want the
fastest LGA1700 chip.

## Pros

- Twenty cores for gaming and heavy multi-threaded work
- Integrated graphics as a fallback display output
- Works with DDR4 or DDR5 boards

## Cons

- Up to 253 W under sustained load: demanding on cooling and the PSU
- No cooler in the box
- LGA1700 has no further CPU generations to come

## FAQ

### Which cooler should I buy?

Not one rated for its 125 W TDP: at full load it draws about 253 W, so choose a cooler rated for at
least 253 W, such as the Coldmark Peak Duo (270 W), the Coldmark Hydro 280 or a 360 mm AIO. Anything
rated lower, including the Breeze Frost 140 Duo (250 W), gets a warning in the PC builder.
Low-profile and AMD-only coolers do not fit or are rated too low.

### What power supply do I need?

With a card up to about 200 W, such as the Halcyon HX 6060 Dual, 750 W. With a 250–265 W card,
850 W. With a 320 W-class card, 1000 W. A 550 W unit is too small with a 260 W-class card such as
the Aurex Nova 70, and the PC builder flags it.

### Does it work in the Meridian B760M Forge D4?

Yes. The board ships with BIOS 16 and Harrier needs BIOS 12. It is a DDR4 board, so pair it with
DDR4 memory.
