## Overview

The Voltaris V9 8950 is the sixteen-core, thirty-two-thread flagship of the Obsidian (AM5)
generation. It boosts to 5.7 GHz, has integrated graphics and is built for rendering, compiling,
simulation and other workloads that use every core. Its rated TDP is 170 W and it draws up to
about 230 W under sustained all-core load.

That power has two consequences. The cooler must be rated for at least 170 W, and a 240 mm or
larger AIO or a large dual-tower air cooler is the sensible choice. The power supply needs real
headroom, especially with a high-end graphics card. Memory is DDR5 only, officially up to
5600 MT/s. Early AM5 boards need a BIOS update before they recognise it.

## Who it's for

Creators, developers and engineers whose work scales with cores, and enthusiasts who want the
fastest AM5 CPU for both work and play.

## Pros

- Sixteen cores for heavy multi-threaded work
- Integrated graphics, handy for workstations and troubleshooting
- Works on any AM5 board with a current BIOS

## Cons

- No cooler in the box; needs a cooler rated for 230 W or more
- High power draw under full load
- Early AM5 boards need a BIOS update first

## FAQ

### Which cooler should I buy?

Anything rated for at least 230 W, its sustained draw, with an AM5 mount. For quiet sustained loads
we recommend the Breeze Frost 140 Duo (250 W) or a 280 mm or 360 mm AIO such as the Coldmark Hydro
280 or Breeze Glacier 360. The Breeze Frost 120 and Breeze Glacier 120 (180 W), the Coldmark Classic
92 (120 W) and the low-profile coolers are rated too low.

### What power supply do I need?

With a graphics card up to about 220 W, 750 W is enough. With a 320 W-class card such as the
Halcyon HX 7080 Triple, choose 1000 W. The PC builder shows the estimated load and headroom.

### Is it good for gaming?

Yes, but games rarely use sixteen cores. If you only game, the V5 8600 or V7 8700 is the better
value.
